**UI/UX Designer: Product Design Assignment**

## Product Context

We are building a Challan Management Platform for two primary customer types:

| Customer Type | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. Fleet companies | Who manage a large number of vehicles and drivers. |
| 2. Leasing companies | Who manage leased vehicles where responsibility for a challan may sit with the driver, lessee, vehicle owner, fleet operator, or leasing company. |

The platform should help customers:

*(blank in the supplied text)*

## Requirements

- Add vehicles manually or through Excel/import.
- Search vehicles using their registration/owner details.
- Fetch vehicles registered to an owner through an API.
- View all challans associated with vehicles.
- Identify the type/status of each challan.
- Determine who is responsible for payment.
- Track whether a challan is paid, assigned, disputed, or pending.
- Handle multiple vehicles, drivers and responsibility scenarios.
- Eventually support payment and workflow management.

## The Design Problem

Do not start by designing screens.

Work out first:

- Who are the different users?
- What problems does each user have?
- What is the journey from start to finish?
- What are the major workflows?
- What are the edge cases?
- What does a fleet or leasing company need to know to make a decision?
- Where can the workflow break?

## Part 4: UI Design

Design the screens needed to demonstrate the core workflow.

Mandatory Screens:

| No. | Screen |
|---|---|
| 1 | Dashboard |
| 2 | Vehicle listing |
| 3 | Vehicle details |
| 4 | Challan listing |
| 5 | Challan details and responsibility assignment |
| 6 | Payment or resolution state |

Screens do not need to be pixel perfect. We want to see whether you can build a product experience that holds together.

## What We Are Asking For, in One Line

Design a Challan Management Platform for fleet and leasing companies, from start to finish, that shows how you understand users, define workflows, identify edge cases, build a scalable UI/UX system, and use AI to work faster without letting it replace your design thinking.

## Part 5: Design system

Set up a basic design system foundation and build your screens on top of it.

We are not asking for an exhaustive system. We are looking at whether you understand the difference between styling screens and creating something reusable.

If you work in Figma, show us:

- Components
- Variants
- Auto layout
- Variables and tokens
- Reusable styles

## Part 6: Walkthrough

Send a walkthrough along with the assignment so we can follow what you did and why.

A screen recording or a short presentation is fine, whichever is quicker for you. It does not need to be polished.

Cover: problem, user, journey, solution, UI, edge cases, and the decisions you made.

You should be able to demonstrate the prototype and explain why you designed it that way.

## AI usage policy

You may use AI tools. We encourage it, and we use them here ourselves.

Use AI for brainstorming, research, copy, exploring alternatives, early ideas, competitor analysis, supporting visuals, and repetitive work.

What we need to see is your own UX thinking, product understanding, design judgement, interaction design and visual design. AI should help you work, not do the work.

If you hand the problem statement to an AI tool and present the screens it produces, you will score poorly.
